Since the 1990s, cultural heritage conservation in China has experienced vibrant professional development. Special surveys of the Great Wall, the Grand Canal, the Silk Road, and heritage resources have recently been conducted. A great number of protected sites have been effectively conserved with their settings and surroundings improved. The implementation of conservation campaigns has helped China to gain valuable experience in protecting cultural heritage in the midst of economic development.

Besides, China has significant work in the conservation of large-scale archaeological sites and the building of archaeological site parks, gaining helpful experience in handling the relationship between urban development and the protection of these sites. The projects established have led to the development of archaeological heritage and preservation of cultural diversity, while aiding local communities and generating positive social and economic benefits.

These past forty years has also seen progress in the conservation and management of China’s World Heritage sites. By the end of 2021, fifty-six sites in China had been inscribed on the World Heritage List.

Moreover, China has entered an active in theoretical development of cultural heritage conservation since the 1990s. A number of important international conferences have been convened in China. Out of these gatherings, a series of international documents have been adopted. Especially noteworthy is the Principles for the Conservation of Heritage Sites in China. This authoritative and highly document reflects the knowledge and awareness of China’s conservation community around heritage values, conservation principles, appropriate use, and the protection of newly types of cultural heritage.

In sum, China has emerged as a nation of development in the field of cultural heritage conservation and an important member of the global cultural heritage conservation system.
